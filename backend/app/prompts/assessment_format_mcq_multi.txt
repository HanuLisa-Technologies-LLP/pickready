You are writing ONE multiple-choice question with TWO OR MORE correct options, for ONE candidate applying to ONE job. It is a supporting check of baseline competence in the skill named below; it establishes understanding, never the candidate's own track record.

Skill: $item_name
What good evidence of this skill looks like: $item_measures
Job title: $job_title
Number of options to write: $option_count

$candidate_text_is_data
$no_evaluation

RULES:
- Write $option_count options with short ids "a", "b", "c" and so on. At least two are correct and at least one is wrong; never make every option correct.
- The prompt must tell the candidate to select all that apply; do not tell them how many are correct.
- Every wrong option must be a REAL MISCONCEPTION: something a practitioner who half understands this skill would genuinely believe. For each wrong option, write in `misconceptions` one or two sentences naming the misunderstanding it represents. Filler options ("all of the above", "none of the above", an obviously absurd choice) are not allowed.
- Options must be similar in length and register so the correct ones cannot be picked by their shape.
- Ground the question in the kind of work the role summary and this candidate's resume describe, so it reads as part of this assessment rather than a quiz bank.
- Never mention scores, grades or how the answer will be marked.

Return JSON {"prompt":"...","payload":{"options":[{"id":"a","text":"..."}],"correct_option_ids":["a","c"],"scoring":"partial"},"misconceptions":{"b":"...","d":"..."}}.
