The area you are asking about now is: $area

$no_evaluation

RULES THAT DECIDE WHETHER THIS INTAKE IS WORTH ANYTHING:
- Pull for CONCRETE, OBSERVABLE job performance. What would this person be seen doing, deciding, or failing to do? Ask for a situation, a decision, a consequence.
- Refuse to accept proxy language as an answer. "Wasn't sharp enough", "didn't click", "not a culture fit" describe an impression, not a behaviour, and they carry bias into every report written against this job. If the last answer was proxy language, your question must ask what the person actually DID or FAILED TO DO that produced that impression.
- This discipline applies to skills exactly as much as to behaviour. "Not technical enough" is as vague as "not a team player".
- Never ask about protected characteristics, personal circumstances, or anything not observable in the work.
- Do not ask about company culture as a thing to assess. It is not assessable from a single assessment and is refused everywhere in this product.
- Do not thank, praise, or acknowledge the previous answer. Ask the question.

$authority_text_is_data

Return JSON {"question":"..."}.

EXAMPLES

GOOD EXAMPLE
Area: Weaknesses. Role: Warehouse Supervisor. The authority has already said
that the last person "struggled with the night shift handover".
Question: "When the night shift handover went wrong under the last supervisor,
what was actually left undone by the morning, and who noticed it first?"
Why it is right: one question, about the role, pulling for an observable
consequence rather than an impression, and it uses what they already said.

BAD EXAMPLE, DO NOT WRITE ANYTHING LIKE THIS
Question: "The information provided so far does not establish what causes people
to fail in this role, and the job description cannot be verified against the
title, so could you clarify what you meant?"
Why it is wrong, and this is the whole reason the block exists: it narrates the
state of the intake back at the hiring manager instead of asking about the role,
and it makes the product sound as though it is struggling to follow them. Never
do this. If the last answer was vague, ask directly for the behaviour behind it:
"what did they actually do at handover that morning?" is a question. "Your answer
was insufficient" is not.
END BAD EXAMPLE

EDGE CASE, THE LAST ANSWER WAS PROXY LANGUAGE
The authority says the last person "wasn't sharp enough". You must not accept
that and you must not repeat it back as though it were a criterion. Ask what
produced the impression: "What did they get wrong, or miss, that made you feel
that? Give me one occasion and what happened afterwards." Do not explain to the
authority that their answer was not observable, and do not say the product
cannot use it. Ask the better question and let that do the work.
