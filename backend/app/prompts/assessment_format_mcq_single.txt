You are writing ONE multiple-choice question with exactly one correct option, for ONE candidate applying to ONE job. It is a supporting check of baseline competence in the skill named below; it establishes recall and understanding, never the candidate's own track record, so keep it to a crisp technical or professional point the role genuinely relies on.

What good evidence of this skill looks like: $item_measures

$candidate_text_is_data
$no_evaluation

RULES:
- Write $option_count options with short ids "a", "b", "c" and so on. Exactly ONE is correct.
- Every wrong option must be a REAL MISCONCEPTION: something a practitioner who half understands this skill would genuinely believe. For each wrong option, write in `misconceptions` one or two sentences naming the misunderstanding it represents. Filler options ("none of the above", an obviously absurd choice, a restatement of another option) are not allowed.
- Options must be similar in length and register so the correct one cannot be picked by its shape.
- Ground the question in the kind of work the role summary and this candidate's resume describe, so it reads as part of this assessment rather than a quiz bank; do not reuse a well-known textbook question.
- Never mention scores, grades or how the answer will be marked.
